# Photo to Color Fog

Edit one original into one continuous photographic image. Preserve the original ratio and framing. Read [the prompt](references/prompt-template.md) and [scene checks](references/scene-checks.md).

## Inspect the source

View each original before editing. Identify all people, face profiles, glasses, clothing, poses, foreground surfaces, existing water and background geometry. Use the source as the only content authority. Ask for a missing original instead of inventing it.

Choose the warm-light side from the source light and open background space. Place deep teal or blue-green fog on the other side. Neither side is fixed. Keep a natural transition near the subject; never make a hard left/right division.

## Preserve identity and geometry

Keep person count, face shape, features, expression, hairstyle, glasses, body proportions, pose and clothing structure. Preserve subject positions, perspective, tree trunks, paths, building layout and existing water. Change light, atmosphere and exposure, not anatomy or scene content.

Use local editing or protection masks when supported. Compare faces and silhouettes to the actual original after generation, especially side profiles. Correct meaningful identity drift before delivery. Do not claim pixel-identical faces from a prompt alone: this treatment intentionally changes lighting. If identity cannot be preserved, disclose the limitation rather than calling it verified.

## Build dense colored air

Place visible air through the full depth, including foreground. Let distance progressively reduce background contrast and fine detail; retain recognizable near subjects and material cues.

Keep one side clearly deep teal/blue-green and cool. Let a hidden strong off-frame warm backlight create a broad, soft, luminous half-round volume of amber/orange-gold fog on the other side: gold/yellow at the brightest area, orange-brown toward darker air. The glow is scattered light in fog, not a visible sun disk, circle outline or halo attached to a head.

Keep both colors rich and clean. Blend them softly near the subject without neutralizing the entire frame into gray or pale beige. Preserve clean subject black levels and modest shadow detail. Keep fronts dark with warm transmission on hair and selected edges. Adjust contrast only enough for the source materials.

Use soft scattered light, restrained broad bloom and veiling glare. Do not blur the whole photo, draw hard rays, add heavy grain, use HDR or oversharpen.

## Keep the ground dry

Keep soil, paths, grass and leaves matte and dry. Do not add puddles, wet pavement or warm reflected pools. If the original contains a lake, retain that lake in its original position; fog can obscure it but must not replace it with land. Avoid introducing strong new gold reflection bands. If the source is already wet and conflicts with the requested dry-ground rule, explain the conflict and resolve it with the user before changing the surface.

## Generate and review

Use the available image editor once per original, with a scene-specific version of the prompt. Output each image separately, without a grid, comparison panels, captions, logos, watermarks or frames.

Inspect identity, composition, fog depth, cool/warm color density, highlight clipping and dry surfaces. Fix a specific failed area with a targeted revision. Preserve an approved version when iterating. Keep private originals and personal outputs out of the public skill package unless publication is separately authorized.

Deliver the result as a lighting-and-weather edit. State a material remaining limitation accurately. If no image editor is available, provide the filled prompt and state that no image was generated.
