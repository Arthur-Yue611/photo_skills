# Source choices and output checks

| Source | Keep | Fog and light adaptation |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Environmental portrait or group | Faces, glasses, count, poses and clothing | Keep bodies dark but recognizable; light only selected hair/edges. |
| Walking back view | Stride, silhouette, path and subject size | Let distant structures fade progressively; do not replace the environment. |
| Forest | Trunk positions, lean, bark and branches | Place warm air through existing gaps; retain cold fog depth elsewhere. |
| Lakeside | Actual lake boundary, shore and people | Keep the shore dry and matte; do not add gold reflection bands. |
| Buildings or streets | Perspective, windows, doors and surface texture | Use existing open space for backlit fog; do not add lamps or wet pavement. |

Check original ratio/crop, identity and positions first. Compare the darkest subject details and brightest glow at normal viewing size. Look for washed-out colors, hard rays, a visible sun disk, lost foreground air, invented objects and wet-looking ground. Keep real background people in their original places; fog may obscure them naturally.

This style works best with visible depth and room for backlight. Close-up faces and plain studio backgrounds provide less space for the effect. Three initial scene trials establish examples, not universal reliability. No automated face-preservation or fog-generation script is included.
