# Scene adaptation

| Photo type | Useful source structure | Color/paper decisions | Watch for |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Neon streets, illuminated paths, night portraits | Light pools, perspective, original silhouettes | Ink shadows; actual light colors bloom in their observed direction; grey paper | Fake smoke, extra lamps, invented faces |
| Autumn foliage and forests | Branch trajectories, canopy gaps, leaf clusters | Source red/yellow/green lead; restrained spectral accents; warm paper | Unrelated rainbow leaves |
| Flowers and gardens | Stems, petal shapes, foreground focus | Flower colors lead; folds follow stems/petals | Losing distinctive species shapes |
| Water and wet pavement | Reflections, ripples, shoreline, real highlights | Blue/cyan lead; quiet violet/warm accents; cool paper | Invented stars instead of water |
| Buildings, windows, trains and shops | Perspective, rails, facade planes | Pigment follows actual planes; few related folds | Invented text/building counts |
| Clothes, flags, textiles and markets | Fabric folds, hanging direction, color blocks | Translate cloth into paper layers; retain color contrast | Extra objects or excessive layers |
| Animals with surroundings | Animal pose, fence/branch/ground relation | Readable original outline; source-led pigment | Changed species or extra limbs |
| Environmental portraits and groups | Gesture, spacing, facing direction | Actual faces above; same poses abstracted below | Tight top crops or invented lower faces |

Adapt these decisions to each source; do not impose one template. References contain day scenes as well as a neon night scene. Do not turn every source into night or add fog that was absent.

Full-spectrum mode is the default required by the seven-color prompt. Some reference examples visibly emphasize only a few colors; retain that restrained weighting with quiet spectral accents. An explicitly chosen source-palette-only variant may omit hues, but must not be mislabeled seven-color.

Prefer wide originals for the 16:9 top. Inspect subject-focused shallow crops for tall originals. The lower art may use the full original for context, while clearly retaining the same scene and relationships.

Use one primary pigment field and a few subordinate folds. Avoid using torn paper as a decorative frame around a miniature photo; transform the scene materially into integrated paper and pigment.
