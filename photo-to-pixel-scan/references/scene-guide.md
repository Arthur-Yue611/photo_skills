# Choose the complete subject and route

| Photo type | Complete subject | Division |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Wide facade, village row or building complex | All connected architecture, including lower walls and stairs | Divide the complete scene; do not isolate the tallest tower. |
| Small isolated building or balloon-shaped attraction | The full visible object | Pass the boundary through the object, then continue to opposing frame edges. |
| Forest, mountain, lake or field | The whole view | Balance the full frame. |
| Portrait, group photo, pet or animal theme | Outside scope | Do not generate this style. |

Keep the entire original view. A castle can be a separate subject when isolated in a wide landscape, or part of a whole-scene division when it fills the frame. Judge its actual extent rather than its category.

Aim to balance the subject and full frame together. For an off-center object, a shallow left-to-right crossing may satisfy both better than a top-to-bottom line. Choose the position and angle for each source; do not copy a previous reference's boundary coordinates.

Keep the route close to a straight overall direction with only tiny smooth waves. Do not bend sharply to follow a roof or make a large curve to chase equal areas. Scene-edge matching is optional and less important than a natural simple route.

Use larger cells on broad forms; use somewhat smaller cells on distant houses, windows, rails, supports and ropes. Smaller does not mean photographic or smoothly painted. Keep all pixel-side objects visibly pixel art.

For a static balloon-shaped attraction, preserve stripes, supports and tethers. Do not invent a basket, flame or airborne setting. Keep a clear sky clear and preserve only source cloud shapes.

For incidental distant people in scenery, preserve count and location without inventing facial detail. If a recognizable face would fall in the edited region, revise the route or request a different source.
